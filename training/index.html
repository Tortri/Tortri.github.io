Principles

  • Minutes and zones, never distance, so the same plan fits any level. Load is counted as minutes × zone (Edwards' zone-weighted heart-rate method).
  • Periodisation: base → build → race-specific → taper → race, sized to the time until your race (up to a year).
  • ~80% easy: most minutes in Z1–2, 1–2 hard sessions a week (Seiler & Kjerland 2006; Stöggl & Sperlich 2014; Casado et al. 2022).
  • Hard day, 2–3 easy days, hard day: intervals and leg strength share a day so easy days stay easy; the long run sits at the end of the week.
  • 3:1 cycles (or 2:1): three building weeks then a ~30% lighter week, so load turns into fitness (supercompensation).
  • Adapts to what you did: weekly increases ≤10%; a much bigger week than your 4-week average (acute:chronic > 1.3) triggers a lighter week; after 1–4+ weeks with little training it restarts at ~85/70/60/50% of your earlier level.
  • Taper: volume cut ~40–60% over 1–2 weeks while intensity and frequency are kept (Bosquet et al. 2007).
  • Strength 2×/week in base/build, 1×/week near the race; plyometrics in hard-day warm-ups; tendon loading 2–3×/week. No static stretching: it doesn't reduce injuries, strength work does (Lauersen et al. 2014).
  • Far future is projected from your season curve and firms up within ~3 weeks; past days are frozen so changes only move the future.

Honest caveats: the 10% rule, the 1.3 acute:chronic threshold and the return-after-break percentages are practical rules of thumb built on the research, not exact laws (see Impellizzeri et al. 2020). Listen to your body; pain that changes how you run is a reason to stop.
